STUDIO EDIT 01
PRODUCT AND DESIGN NOTES

Ideas worth acting on

This digest is a practical creative prompt for product conversations. It does not make market forecasts. Use the questions that fit your project, then confirm the product details in a shared specification.

1. BUILD A CLUB CAPSULE AROUND ONE ANCHOR

A small coordinated assortment can start with one garment or graphic that carries the strongest identity. Choose the piece that sets the tone. Let other items support it through a related color story, consistent placement or a repeated detail.

Before sampling, list each garment, the decoration location, artwork version, and color and size mix. If an emblem appears on more than one item, note whether its dimensions change. A mark that reads well on a large back print may need another treatment on a cap or patch.

Questions to take into a brief:
- Which item anchors the group?
- Which design details repeat, and which vary?
- What size and color options are needed?
- Where should each decoration sit?

2. LET THE SURFACE DO SOME OF THE WORK

Fabric, thread, knit structure and patch construction affect how a product looks and feels. A textured surface may call for simpler decoration, while a flatter surface can leave more room for fine lines or small lettering. The useful choice depends on artwork scale and intended use.

Share a close reference for any surface detail that matters. Identify the finished size of lettering and narrow shapes. For patches, compare matching construction references: embroidered, woven, chenille and PVC have different surfaces and should be reviewed as distinct options.

Sample review prompts:
- Does the color remain legible against the base?
- Are small letters and narrow spaces clear at finished size?
- Does the chosen construction suit the artwork?
- Does the edge or border work with the intended attachment?

3. DESIGN AROUND THE HEADWEAR PANEL

A cap front is curved and divided by seams. The panel shape, profile and decoration placement affect how a graphic reads. Keep the primary mark clear at finished size, and account for seams and curves in the placement reference.

For a useful review, share the cap profile, closure preference, color, artwork size and approximate front placement. A simple mockup can help compare the mark with the center seam and panel shape before approving a sample.

Details to record:
- Cap or hat profile and panel construction
- Closure and fit requirements
- Decoration size and placement
- Fabric color and thread or print references

4. FINISH THE FULL PRESENTATION

Packaging and labels are easier to plan when they are part of the product brief. The right format depends on how items are stored, shipped, grouped or presented. Confirm the packing unit and destination so protection and labeling requirements can be considered with the product.

Questions to answer early:
- Is each item packed individually or in a group?
- Are labels, inserts or product information needed?
- Does the item need protection from folding, moisture or abrasion?
- What destination and delivery instructions apply?
